# Building a Scenario

Version 0.1 (draft). Guidance for anyone designing a Wartable scenario: how big the armies should be for a map, and how long the game should run. These are rules of thumb, not rules: playtest and adjust.

## What a scenario sets

Every scenario states:

- **Map:** size, terrain (core rules 2.3), and objectives.
- **Deployment zones** for each side, and the default facing on deployment.
- **Capacity:** the total Cost each side may field.
- **Units allowed.**
- **Win conditions** and a **turn limit**.
- **Tie-break:** who holds it at the start.

## Sizing the armies

### 1. Measure the frontage

The **frontage (F)** is how many hexes wide the line is where the armies will meet: usually the width of the map across the direction of advance, or the length of a river or ridge both sides will fight over.

### 2. Units per side

| Kind of battle | Units per side |
| --- | --- |
| Open: room to manoeuvre and to flank | F ÷ 3 |
| Packed: a line fight with little room | F ÷ 2 |

### 3. Capacity

Work out two figures and use the **higher**:

- From frontage: **units per side × 15**. Fifteen is the average Cost of the standard units (Infantry 10, Cavalry 20, Artillery 15); use your own average if your units differ.
- From space: aim for about **15 hexes per unit**, so **units per side ≈ map hexes ÷ 30**, then × 15.

Round to the nearest 10. Frontage alone underestimates maps with deep rear areas or several objectives: playtesting Two Towns at the frontage figure (120) felt too thin to both hold a line and attack, and the space figure (240) is what it needed.

### 4. Check the space

After choosing capacity, divide the map's hexes by the total number of units on both sides:

| Hexes per unit | Feel |
| --- | --- |
| Under 10 | Cramped: little manoeuvre, fights start at once |
| 10–20 | Normal |
| Over 25 | Too thin: lots of marching, open flanks, armies that can't both defend and attack. Raise capacity |

### 5. Turn limit

**Turn limit ≈ turns to march to the enemy's objective + 10.** Turns to march = distance between the deployment zone and the enemy's objective ÷ infantry Move (4), plus 1 for each river that must be forded. The extra 10 turns are for the fighting.

### 6. Starting limit and bank (optional)

A **starting limit** below capacity (about half) puts the rest in the bank, to be bought during the game (core rules, section 10). It keeps a reserve for both sides to react with, and lets a defender rush units to a threatened objective.

## Worked examples

| | Starter Battle | Two Towns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map | 12 × 10 = 120 hexes | 26 × 18 = 468 hexes |
| Frontage | 12 (map width) | 18 (the river, full height) |
| Units per side | frontage 4–6, space 4 | frontage 6–9, space 16 |
| Capacity | frontage 60–90, space 60 → **60** | frontage 90–135, space 230 → **240** |
| Hexes per unit | 120 ÷ 8 = **15** | 468 ÷ 32 ≈ **15** |
| March to objective | 8 ÷ 4 = 2 turns | 23 ÷ 4 ≈ 6 turns (by bridge) |
| Turn limit | 2 + 10 = **12** | 6 + 10 = **16** |

## Balance checks

- **Measure key distances** from each side's deployment zone to each objective and crossing. Uneven distances aren't wrong, but know them, and watch them in playtesting.
- **Give each side a reason to move.** Objectives worth more than a few units' Cost stop both players waiting behind their artillery.
- **Count crossings and choke points.** Two bridges on an 18-hex river makes them the centre of the battle; add fords or bridges if every game jams at one point.
